# Two-minute judge demo

**0:00–0:15 — Mission Control.** Introduce the experience: “This is a local-first astronaut trainer for ages 8–14. Every interaction introduces a real space-science idea.” Point out rank, XP, mission progress, and the live-feed cards.

**0:15–0:40 — Launch Sequence.** Open the launch mission, verify the five system checks, and start the countdown. Explain that the checklist mirrors the systems thinking behind real preflight procedures.

**0:40–1:05 — Orbit Mechanics.** Move the speed and altitude controls into the stable zone. Explain that the ISS travels about 7.7 km/s at roughly 400 km, and that the visualization teaches the concept rather than calculating a precision orbit.

**1:05–1:25 — Mars Rover.** Show the NASA rover image, navigate the grid around obstacles, and point out its rover/camera/sol metadata and NASA API attribution.

**1:25–1:42 — Mission Control Quiz.** Choose an answer and read the explanation: astronauts float because they and the ISS are in free fall together, not because gravity disappears.

**1:42–2:00 — Accessibility, offline, and reward.** Show the keyboard-friendly missions and high-contrast/reduced-motion toggles, then the crew name field and downloadable certificate. Close with the local progress and cached app shell that keep core missions available offline.
